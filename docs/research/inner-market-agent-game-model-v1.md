# Inner Market Participant Game Model — V1

- Status: Research model
- Version: 0.1
- Scope: Pump.fun bonding-curve phase
- Purpose: Model heterogeneous participant incentives and their observable interactions for defensive simulation
- Safety boundary: Offline simulation only; no live manipulation or execution policies

## 1. Central market conflict

The most important inner-market conflict is the interaction between:

1. concentrated early inventory and potential future supply;
2. independent external demand and its ability to absorb available supply;
3. the bonding curve's state-dependent price sensitivity;
4. uncertainty about who controls related wallets and who is acting independently.

The simulator must not assume that a rising price means broad demand, that a high volume means independent participation, or that a large holder intends to sell.

## 2. Shared state

At time t, agents observe some or all of the public market state:

S_t = (X_t, Y_t, X_real,t, Y_real,t, P_t, Q_t, H_t, E_t, G_t)

Where:

- X_t, Y_t: virtual quote and token reserves.
- X_real,t, Y_real,t: real reserve state under the program's accounting semantics.
- P_t: current curve price.
- Q_t: curve progress / completion state.
- H_t: wallet-level inventory ledger.
- E_t: ordered execution and transfer event history.
- G_t: evidence graph connecting addresses through observable relationships.

Not every agent observes the full state. The simulator should represent information asymmetry and delayed / incomplete observations.

## 3. Agent heterogeneity

Each agent has a parameter vector θ_i. It may include:

- initial inventory and quote-asset budget;
- risk tolerance;
- time horizon;
- information latency;
- execution latency;
- slippage tolerance;
- sensitivity to price momentum;
- sensitivity to social information;
- probability of holding, buying, or selling;
- degree of independence from other agents.

Agent decisions are generated by a stochastic policy:

a_i,t ~ π_i(S_i,t, θ_i, ε_i,t)

where S_i,t is the information actually available to agent i and ε_i,t is a random behavioral shock.

The simulation must use heterogeneous distributions rather than one deterministic policy for every wallet. It must record the random seed and agent parameters for reproducibility.

## 4. Participant roles and defensive observables

| Role | Incentive / constraint | Observable signatures | Confounders |
|---|---|---|---|
| Issuer / Dev | Has launch context, may retain inventory, may receive creator-related fees depending on program configuration | Creator metadata, directly identified inventory, authority state, creator fee flows | Creator wallet may not represent all related ownership |
| Early holder | Holds supply acquired or received near launch | Early acquisition, holding duration, later inventory changes | Early participation is not proof of insider status |
| Sniper | Values execution speed and early entry | Early-slot concentration, short holding duration, high early turnover | Legitimate fast participants and bots can look similar |
| Retail buyer | Heterogeneous information and risk tolerance | Smaller varied trades, delayed response, diverse holding periods | Retail can arrive in synchronized bursts after public information |
| KOL follower | Responds to public calls or social information | Time-correlated entry after a public signal | Correlation can be organic and should not be treated as wallet coordination |
| Organic seller | Reduces exposure or exits | Inventory decrease and sell-side quote proceeds | Portfolio rebalancing can resemble coordinated selling |
| Arbitrage / searcher | Responds to price discrepancies | Short-lived inventory, cross-market convergence, high turnover | High gross/net divergence is not proof of wash activity |
| MEV actor | Benefits from transaction ordering and inclusion opportunities | Ordering patterns, execution-price displacement, victim-price impact | Network congestion and priority-fee competition |
| Data observer | Reconstructs and classifies market events | Features, evidence, confidence, counter-evidence | Incomplete indexing and ambiguous wallet identity |

A participant may change roles over time. Role labels are latent simulation metadata and must not be exposed to the detector during ordinary evaluation.

## 5. Dev privilege model

Represent the Dev's observable privilege set as:

D_t = (I_direct,t, I_associated,t, A_t, F_creator,t, Info_t)

- I_direct,t: inventory in directly identified issuer / creator addresses.
- I_associated,t: estimated inventory in addresses with relationship evidence, with confidence bounds.
- A_t: relevant authorities and launch configuration.
- F_creator,t: creator-related fee flows where the program configuration supports them.
- Info_t: privileged information category, represented only as a simulation variable.

Separate three classes of attribution:

1. Directly verified control or authority.
2. Probable association supported by multiple independent evidence types.
3. Unknown ownership.

Never collapse these categories into one “Dev supply” number. Do not infer that early buyers, common funders, or synchronized wallets are necessarily controlled by the issuer.

## 6. Interaction model

The bonding curve couples every participant through a shared state transition:

S_(t+1) = F_program(S_t, ordered_events_t, fee_config_t)

The same event can affect multiple dimensions simultaneously:

- curve price and reserve state;
- token inventory held by each participant;
- quote-asset balances and fees;
- holder concentration;
- observed OHLCV;
- curve progress and graduation status.

This coupling is why independent sub-models are insufficient. Price, volume, and holder distribution must be replayed from the same ordered event ledger.

## 7. Key interaction hypotheses

### H1. Early inventory versus independent demand

A token can have a rising curve price while a large share of supply remains concentrated among early participants. Price appreciation alone does not establish broad ownership distribution.

Measure:
- direct and probable early inventory separately;
- independent buyer breadth proxies;
- inventory changes over time;
- transfer activity separately from buys and sells;
- uncertainty in wallet attribution.

### H2. Sniper activity versus organic discovery

Fast early entries can create concentrated activity. The same visual pattern can also arise from legitimate bots, experienced traders, or public launch notifications.

Measure:
- slot / transaction-order concentration;
- entry-time distribution;
- holding-time distribution;
- buyer breadth;
- post-entry inventory changes.

Do not label a wallet as a sniper or insider from one feature alone.

### H3. Social information versus coordinated wallets

Public KOL or community information can cause many unrelated wallets to trade within a short time window. Temporal correlation is therefore weak evidence by itself.

Measure:
- whether funding and transaction counterparties are also related;
- whether inventory is retained independently;
- whether the same addresses repeatedly co-move across unrelated tokens;
- the share of activity that remains unclassified.

### H4. Arbitrage turnover versus independent demand

Arbitrage can create substantial gross turnover while net inventory changes remain small. It may also improve price consistency across markets.

Measure:
- gross quote turnover;
- wallet-group inventory changes;
- cross-market price basis;
- holding duration;
- convergence after price discrepancies.

Treat arbitrage as a distinct class, not automatically as suspicious volume.

### H5. Price shock versus durable price discovery

A short-lived price displacement can dominate candle high / low even if independent participation is limited.

Measure:
- event count and participant breadth;
- impact normalized by virtual-reserve depth;
- persistence across multiple horizons;
- reserve reconciliation;
- whether other markets confirm the price.

Preserve the raw candle and add an integrity overlay.

### H6. Early-holder inventory versus realized sell pressure

A large early holder balance is potential supply, not realized sell pressure. Actual sell pressure requires observed inventory reduction through market sells, and attribution may remain uncertain.

Measure:
- current balances and balance deltas;
- market sells versus transfers;
- quote proceeds;
- related-party confidence;
- available curve state.

## 8. Phase-dependent observation model

| Phase | Agent mix and uncertainty | Primary defensive questions |
|---|---|---|
| Launch | Small sample, early holders and fast participants dominate observations | What is known about initial allocation, authorities, and first funding paths? |
| Early curve | High sensitivity to relative trade size; limited behavioral baseline | Is price action broad or concentrated? How uncertain is wallet independence? |
| Middle curve | More participants and more history | Is gross turnover supported by independent inventory accumulation? |
| Near graduation | Curve completion and remaining inventory become critical | Are curve state, remaining inventory, and holder concentration reconciled? |
| Graduation transition | State changes and indexing delays can occur | Are pre- and post-graduation records separated and reconciled? |

These are analysis phases, not a playbook for participant execution.

## 9. Market integrity vector

Do not reduce market quality to one scalar in V1. Publish a vector:

M_t = (D_t, B_t, C_t, I_t, O_t, R_t)

- D_t: independent-demand proxies and net inventory accumulation.
- B_t: effective buyer / seller breadth.
- C_t: holder and flow concentration.
- I_t: price impact relative to curve depth and its persistence.
- O_t: potential inventory overhang, with attribution uncertainty.
- R_t: data completeness and curve-state reconciliation.

Every dimension must show source data, confidence, and counter-evidence.

## 10. False-positive test cases

The detector must not over-flag:

- many independent buyers responding to one public KOL post;
- unrelated wallets funded by the same exchange hot wallet;
- legitimate arbitrage with high gross turnover;
- bots that provide execution speed but do not share ownership;
- early holders selling independently;
- a genuine news-driven price jump;
- a short-lived price move caused by ordinary low liquidity;
- RPC/indexer duplication or delayed events.

## 11. V1 acceptance criteria

- [ ] Agents have heterogeneous parameters and reproducible random seeds.
- [ ] Public information and privileged simulation-only information are separated.
- [ ] Agent role labels are hidden from the detector during evaluation.
- [ ] All agent events update one shared, ordered market-state ledger.
- [ ] Dev inventory attribution distinguishes direct, probable, and unknown ownership.
- [ ] KOL-driven correlated activity is included as a legitimate false-positive case.
- [ ] Arbitrage and MEV are modeled separately from circular-flow scenarios.
- [ ] Potential sell overhang is separated from realized market selling.
- [ ] Market integrity is reported as a vector with confidence, not a single opaque score.
- [ ] No live execution, wallet orchestration, or manipulation-optimization objective is present.
